My Football Journey

My football story began like many others, with a ball, a dream and a pair of boots that were far too big for me. I was six years old when I joined my first team. At the time, I had no idea how deeply football would shape my mindset, friendships and leadership in the years ahead.

Bjarg

This is where it all began. I was a young boy learning how to kick a ball without falling over.

It did not take long for football to become more serious. When I was seven, my father became the coach of my team. One weekly session soon became four sessions with the team, alongside individual practice in my spare time.

A year later, I was invited to train with older players. The opportunity gave me confidence and motivated me to work even harder.

Brann

At 13, I was invited to join Brann’s youth academy. Receiving that message remains one of the happiest moments of my life.

Joining Brann’s youth academy was my first experience of elite football. The standard was high, and I loved it.

At that point, football occupied nearly every part of my life. I trained before school, attended classes, trained again after school, completed my homework and went to sleep. There was little time for anything else.

The work was demanding, but it was also deeply rewarding. I was living my dream at a young age through major tournaments, high-pressure matches, victories over leading European clubs and travel with the team.

During my three years at Brann, I played alongside athletes who later represented the national team. We won tournaments and became known as one of the best teams in Norway for our age group.

That chapter eventually came to an end. At 16, I received the hardest message of my life. I was no longer guaranteed a place in the team.

Fana

At Fana, I focused on development, learned patience and worked through several injury setbacks.

After leaving Brann, I wanted to find a club where I could continue developing. Before long, I was playing for three teams at Fana. These were the under-19 team, the B team and the A team, although I spent much of my time with the A team on the bench.

In one season, I completed more than 70 full matches and gained valuable experience. I still wanted to become a professional, so I maintained the same demanding routine through a strict diet, individual practice and high expectations. The effort was challenging, rewarding and enjoyable.

Fana also taught me what leadership requires. Greater experience brought greater responsibility. While I learned from my teammates in the A team, I also captained both the under-19 team and the B team. I absorbed lessons from my peers and passed them on to younger players.

Looking back, my time at Fana may have been the period when I grew most, both as a player and as a person beyond football.

Towards the end of my time at Fana, I decided to apply to Ivy League universities in the United States. The application process required a highlight video, which you can watch below.

University of Michigan

During my exchange semester at the University of Michigan, I rediscovered why I love football. Over six months, I played alongside remarkable people. This time, the goal was to enjoy the game rather than to be the best.

I played for two teams at Michigan, the university club team and the MBA team. I formed lifelong friendships, enjoyed countless memorable experiences and played simply for the joy of the game.

Fyllingsdalen

After returning from exchange, I did not have a club to which I could comfortably return. At that point, I considered ending my football career.

Friends at Fyllingsdalen convinced me to attend a trial. I joined the B team in the fifth division and was soon invited to train with the A team as well.

The B team ultimately became my home at Fyllingsdalen, and I was named captain. The squad contained many promising young players. We had considerable talent, but we lacked experience.

Lyngbø

Just as I was close to giving up football, I received a call from Lyngbø. Their ambition was clear. They wanted to win the league, and they wanted me to help them do it.

I gave football one final chance, and the decision created memories that will last a lifetime. I played every match, became part of a close-knit team and found joy in football again.

I am now playing at the highest level of my career. Our supporters, the Ultras, sing our names and create an exceptional atmosphere at every match. The stakes are high, the experience is unforgettable and the story continues.

From the youth system at SK Brann to winning the league with Lyngbø IL, every chapter offered a different lesson. Brann introduced me to elite youth football, while Lyngbø taught me how to support a team through victories and setbacks.

“Football taught me to fight for others, not just for myself.”

I still carry that team-first mentality with me, whether I'm working on a group project or helping lead a student organisation. Football was never only about goals. It was about resilience, community and growth.

These days, I no longer train five times a week. I still bring the same discipline and energy to everything I do, from auditing energy companies at EY to creating impact through student-led events.
